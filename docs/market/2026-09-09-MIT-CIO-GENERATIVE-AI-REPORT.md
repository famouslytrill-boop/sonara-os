# A CIO report whose numbers predate its own subject — 9 September 2026

Checked: 2026-09-09
Review by: 2027-03-09

A link to `ebook_mit-cio-generative-ai-report.pdf` on databricks.com was
submitted. This records what it is, what in it is checkable, and why almost none
of it can go near SONARA's customer-facing copy.

## What it is

**"The great acceleration: CIO perspectives on generative AI."** An **MIT
Technology Review Insights** report, **sponsored by Databricks**, 24 pages.
The back matter reads **"© Copyright MIT Technology Review Insights, 2023. All
rights reserved."**

The report states its own evidence base in the preface, and that is the most
important thing in it:

- **seven in-depth interviews** with senior executives, conducted **April and May
  2023**; and
- a global survey of **600 senior data and technology executives, conducted May
  and June 2022**.

## The dating problem, which is not a small one

**The report's quantitative backbone was collected before the thing the report is
about.** Every percentage in it comes from a survey run in May–June 2022. The
report does not hide this — it labels the charts "2022" and says plainly, in its
own words, that this was *"before the business applications of generative AI
became apparent in late 2022."*

So a document titled *CIO perspectives on generative AI* rests its numbers on
people surveyed before generative AI was a boardroom subject. The interviews are
from 2023 and do speak to it; the statistics do not.

And the second date: **it is now September 2026.** The report is three years old
and its survey is over four. In a subject that moved this fast, quoting a 2022
adoption figure as current would be wrong twice over — once for age, once for
what it was measuring.

## The population is not our market

Six hundred **senior data and technology executives at large enterprises**.
SONARA sells to founders, creators and small teams — the homepage says so
explicitly, "without an enterprise budget or an enterprise maze."

A statistic about what enterprise CIOs planned in 2022 says nothing checkable
about what a two-person business needs in 2026. Using one in our copy would be a
category error sitting on top of a staleness error.

## Which numbers are actually the report's own

These come from its 2022 survey of 600 executives, and are the only figures it
generated rather than cited:

| Finding | Figure |
|---|---|
| Organizations using AI in some way | **94%** |
| Aiming for "enterprise-wide AI" by 2025 | **14%** |
| Saying AI was critical to three or more business functions | **8%** |
| Viewing a unified data platform for analytics and AI as crucial | **nearly 70%** |
| Top tangible benefit noted to date: security and risk management | **31%** |
| Then fraud detection / cyber security / risk management | **27% / 27% / 26%** |

One caution on a chart: Figure 5 carries three agree-statements and three
percentages (**68%, 72%, 78%**), and the text extraction does not reliably show
which percentage belongs to which statement. They are therefore **not** recorded
here as attributed figures. If one is ever needed, read it off the page.

## The biggest numbers in it are borrowed

Several headline figures are **citations, not findings**, and attributing them to
this report would be wrong:

- **McKinsey** — generative AI adding **$2.6–4.4 trillion** in annual value, raising
  AI's economic impact by **15–40%**; and automating activities taking **60–70%** of
  worker time, up from 50%.
- **Accenture** — **40%** of working hours across industries could be automated or
  augmented.
- **Goldman Sachs** — two-thirds of U.S. occupations affected; global GDP up **7%**.

Anything from this list is quoted second-hand. The register rule applies: trace a
statistic to its original source before repeating it, and never cite it as this
report's.

## Where the sponsor shows

Worth naming, because it is the difference between research and content
marketing:

- The finding that a **unified data platform** is crucial is the conclusion most
  favourable to a company that sells one.
- Databricks' own model, **Dolly**, gets a passage — 6 billion parameters, "less
  than 3.5% of the 175 billion used in GPT-3."
- Two of the seven interviewees are Databricks' CTO and a founding advisor to
  MosaicML, which Databricks acquired.

The report describes itself as editorially independent, and that claim is
recorded here rather than assessed. The sponsorship is a fact about the document
either way, and a reader deserves it alongside the findings.

## What can honestly be taken

One observation, and it is a shape rather than a number.

**Before generative AI, almost nobody had joined AI up across a business.** 14%
aspired to enterprise-wide AI; 8% had it critical in three or more functions.
Whatever the figures are now, the *shape* — capability bolted on in one or two
places rather than running through the operation — is the condition SONARA's
"one record, not three" positioning addresses.

That is worth knowing while building. It is **not** a marketing claim, because
the evidence for it is a four-year-old survey of a market we do not sell to.

## What must not happen

- **No sentence, heading, table or chart from this PDF in SONARA's product,
  marketing or documentation.** All rights reserved; a free download is a price
  of zero, not a licence.
- **No figure from it in customer-facing copy**, even attributed — it fails our
  own comparison rule, which requires a figure be current, sourced, and about the
  population being described. This one is none of the three.
- **No borrowed statistic repeated as this report's.**
