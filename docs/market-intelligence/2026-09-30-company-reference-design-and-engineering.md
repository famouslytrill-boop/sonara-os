# SONARA Company-Reference Design and Engineering Research

**Research snapshot:** 2026-09-30 (America/New_York)  
**Audience:** founders, creators, small-business operators, and small teams  
**Scope:** public-facing design patterns and operational product behavior across technology, commerce, entertainment, media, automotive, mobility, restaurants, and connected devices. This is a source-grounded desk study, not a usability study or a claim about private company architecture.

## Executive read

The strongest transferable idea is **one coherent platform with clear task-specific products**. Apple, Google, Shopify, Stripe, and Airbnb provide useful first-party material on purpose, adaptive interfaces, repeatable components, and trustworthy task flows. Media and interactive brands show how discovery, emotion, identity, and feedback can make experiences memorable. Retail, restaurants, vehicles, transportation, and connected screens put a harder demand on the software: users need to see availability, price, permission, current state, and next action without guessing.

SONARA should use one accessible interaction contract and three unmistakably different product experiences. **Business Builder** should feel like an operations tool, **Creator Studio** like a protected creative project workspace, and **Growth Studio** like a measured campaign and follow-up workspace. Each product needs its own landing page, copy, logo asset, accent, examples, primary workflow, and dashboard emphasis. Identity, tenant boundaries, billing truth, route conventions, audit, accessibility, and service contracts stay shared.

The moat will not come from dark gradients, cinematic animation, or a long list of integrations. It comes from clear product boundaries, reliable workflow completion, portable user-owned records, evidence of rights and consent, transparent readiness, and provider integrations that are actually authorized and tested.

## Evidence standard and limits

- Prefer first-party design documentation, developer documentation, support material, and current official product descriptions.
- Treat press articles and product pages as evidence of what a company publicly offers, not proof of its internal architecture or user outcomes.
- Translate observations into general interaction principles. Do not copy logos, characters, mascots, exact compositions, text, source code, distinctive trade dress, or proprietary media.
- Company and product names below identify research subjects only. They do not imply partnership, endorsement, API permission, or runtime availability.
- Public-source review cannot establish that a design is usable for SONARA's specific customers. Validate proposed flows through observed customer tasks, route-level tests, accessibility checks, and production telemetry.
- Search-result snippets and rankings are not used as authoritative measures of design quality, company performance, or popularity.

## Findings translated into design rules

### 1. Separate the brands; standardize the contract

The parent brand explains the portfolio. SONARA One explains how the platform connects the work. Each child product has its own landing page and app setup. Reuse accessibility behavior, identity, tenant selection, billing, permissions, navigation conventions, and component states. Do not put all three jobs on one overstuffed dashboard.

### 2. Design around real states, not decorative dashboard tiles

Use the existing state vocabulary (`idle`, `loading`, `streaming`, `partial`, `empty`, `error`, `offline`, `syncing`, `blocked`, `approval_required`, `complete`). Show source, freshness, owner, and next step when useful. Empty or setup-required screens need an explanation and a real destination. A button that cannot name a route or operation is not ready to ship.

### 3. Make every sensitive outcome reviewable

Before publishing, sending a campaign, charging, refunding, dispatching, changing access, or deleting data, show the target, content or record, expected effect, provider and current state. Preserve explicit human approval for consequential actions. After execution, show the provider result, timestamp, correlation/reference ID where appropriate, and retry or reconciliation path.

### 4. Treat accessibility and speed as product quality

Support keyboard and touch, visible focus, meaningful labels, zoom/reflow, reduced motion, captions/transcripts, and non-color-only status. Preserve a non-drag operation for drag actions. Responsive behavior should follow the available component/container space as well as viewport size. Respect media and device capabilities and preserve task completion on a simple 2D path.

### 5. Make resilience visible without exposing implementation jargon

The UI should truthfully represent loading, partial results, offline, queued, failed, retrying, needs setup, and recovered. A retry is safe only when the server-side mutation and downstream effect have a duplicate-safe contract. Do not display guessed progress or mark a payment, upload, publish, or dispatch complete because a spinner ended.

### Route and button acceptance rule

Before adding a control, name its owner, route, HTTP operation or local reversible action, authorization rule, input/output record, user-visible states, error/recovery path, and meaningful test. The existing route registry and `docs/CAPABILITY_ROUTE_SCHEMA_COVERAGE.md` remain the route/schema inventory. Update those inventories when a real route, page, or operation changes; do not create aliases that lead to duplicate experiences.

## Backend and operating-system implications

SONARA One is an application platform, not a general-purpose kernel or a claim of replacing operating systems, consoles, vehicle controllers, or enterprise suites. Product “engines” need accountable inputs, outputs, permission checks, failure behavior, and observable status.

- **Identity and tenant boundary:** server-validated session and role, organization scope on every read/write, database RLS, no browser-held service credentials.
- **Canonical records:** PostgreSQL/Supabase remains the system of record for users' business, growth, and creator data. Use explicit schemas, tenant ownership, migrations, indexes, retention, and deletion behavior; do not create shadow copies to support cosmetic dashboards.
- **Capability plane:** expose real product capabilities through existing registries and entitlement checks. A connection listed in research is not an installed or authorized connector.
- **External providers:** server-side adapters with narrowly scoped credential references, explicit consent/scopes, timeout and rate limits, provider event verification, bounded retries, idempotency, audit, disconnect and data-removal behavior.
- **Jobs and media:** persist job state and correlation IDs; separate original user assets from derivatives; offer resumable/cancelled/failed states; bound CPU/GPU, storage, duration, and cost. Preserve explicit transcript, ownership, source, license, consent, and export metadata.
- **Payments and fulfilment:** provider-authoritative payment state; no raw card/CVV storage; separate pending, succeeded, failed, refunded, disputed, and reconciled states. Orders/jobs/bookings need conflict and cancellation states.
- **Observability and recovery:** request/trace correlation, redacted logs, metrics and alert history, backup/restore evidence, retries with ceilings, dead-letter or reconciliation queue, runbooks, SLOs based on measured service.
- **Public communication:** state what works now, what requires setup, what is in preview, and what depends on third parties. Do not claim “fully current”, “all connected”, “AI-powered”, “instant”, or “unlimited” without testable evidence.

## Build sequence

1. **Brand and route truth:** treat the brand registry and route registry as authority; resolve palette cascade drift before adjusting the production palette. Verify each parent, platform, and product link.
2. **Three product landing pages:** align headline, value, proof, sample output, setup requirements, primary CTA, and product-specific footer/navigation with the product loop above.
3. **Shared interaction foundation:** use common button/form/dialog/table/status/notification/error patterns, keyboard focus and responsive rules; test each product's actual rendered screens.
4. **Real record workflows:** complete one vertically sliced, persistent workflow in each product. Include empty, loading, success, error, permission-limited, and mobile states.
5. **Safe integrations:** prioritize one real connector per product family only after authentication, consent/scopes, tenant isolation, replay safety, lifecycle, monitoring, and recovery are proven.
6. **Operational evidence:** exact-head CI, API and RLS tests, keyboard/screen-reader/zoom checks, responsive screenshots, Lighthouse/Core Web Vitals, load/failure tests, backup/restore, security scan, staged deployment, live-route verification.

The design/priority formula already present in `lib/sonara-frontend-visual-intelligence-2026.cjs` remains the prioritizer:

```text
0.24 × task frequency
+ 0.22 × operational criticality
+ 0.20 × platform reuse
+ 0.18 × mobile importance
+ 0.16 × revenue or service impact
− 0.10 × implementation risk
− 0.10 × interaction risk
```

Use evidence-based 0–1 inputs. The score ranks candidate work; it does not override security, accessibility, owner approval, customer evidence, or release gates.
